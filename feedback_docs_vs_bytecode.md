**"Judge us by our actions (zkProofs), not by our words (documentation)."**

The one-sentence statement of the method. Keep it.

The distinction is not actions versus words, because actions can be staged and still require you to judge intent. It is **verification that needs no trust in the source, versus a claim that does.** Documentation asks you to believe the author. A proof asks you to check, and it holds whether or not you trust whoever produced it.

**Why it is load-bearing beyond Loopring:** it is the same move in every domain that has worked. Deployed bytecode over published repo. Dated behavioral sequence over stated reason. Live chain state over official description. The artifact that executes outranks the artifact that describes.

**Where the instrument mis-fires:** it assumes a structure exists under every stated account. Sometimes the stated thing is the whole thing and nothing sits underneath it. The detector then returns "something is concealed" when the correct output is "nothing is here." See [[feedback-precision-over-helpfulness]].
