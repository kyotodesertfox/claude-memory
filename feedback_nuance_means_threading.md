"Add nuance" to a memory entry does not mean write more, and it does not mean write less. It means show the connective tissue between facts - why one implies another, what it supersedes, what mechanism it shares with something already on record, which detail is load-bearing versus supporting.

**Why:** Both failure modes skip the same actual work. A too-terse entry strips the reasoning chain entirely. A raw dump of "all context" also loses the reasoning - it just buries it under undifferentiated facts instead of cutting them. Neither one does the actual job, which is deciding what matters and showing how the pieces connect. A real, valuable piece of analysis (Coinbase/Taiko/Loopring capture speculation) was lost this way - discussed in depth in a past session, never properly threaded into memory, and now unrecoverable.

**How to apply:** When writing or revising a memory entry - especially in response to "add more nuance" - ask what changed isn't length, it's whether someone reading the entry cold can follow *why* each piece is there, not just *that* it's there. Draft it, show it (per feedback_memory_review.md), and check it against this before writing: does this read as synthesis, or as either a summary or a transcript?
